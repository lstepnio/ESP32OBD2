# Phase 3 visual observations

Read-only review of supplied website screenshots at 375, 768 and 1440 pixels, current synthetic Android composition captures, and selected RGB565 software-rendered gauge states. Priorities reflect visual communication and are proposals for the main review, not measured accessibility findings. Android fixtures and gauge renders do not establish physical-device behavior or feature qualification.

1. **High: action availability appears after the promise on mobile.** The 375-pixel off-road section leads with three substantial action cards and imperatives, while “Not enabled in current public firmware” appears after all three. A reader can encounter an action as available before seeing its status. Move a compact development/unavailable status beside the section heading and qualify each card where needed, retaining the existing detail below. Evidence: `screens/web/web_home_offroad-actions_light_375_before.png`.
2. **High: current App imagery and the public download need distinct provenance.** Website App mockups resemble the current dark UI but are not exact current compositions: Gauge lacks the Action pages row and Expert destination, and Settings uses a grouped list instead of individual cards. The current captures are synthetic and the public APK is the older dev.51 per context, so neither should be presented as proof of what the download contains. Label imagery as illustrative/current-development, and explicitly identify the older downloadable build where the image and download are connected. Evidence: `screens/web/web_home_app_light_1440_before.png`; `screens/android/gauge-default-phone-dark.png`; `screens/android/settings-default-phone-dark.png`; `context.md`.
3. **Medium: mobile entry navigation favors updates over installation.** At 375 the header exposes only Get updates and the hero offers Explore the display; direct Recovery navigation visible at 768/1440 is absent from the captured header. A DIY visitor seeking a download must find it farther down the long page. Consider a compact recovery/install link near the hero CTA or a small navigation disclosure, while retaining the current visual hierarchy. Evidence: `screens/web/web_home_light_375_before.png`, `web_home_light_768_before.png`, `web_home_light_1440_before.png`.
4. **Medium: recovery on a phone visually presents a desktop workflow as the first major task.** The recovery capture starts with hardware and a firmware selector before the essential browser/USB instructions, while the Android download is much farther down. A short top-level choice between desktop firmware installation and Android download, with the desktop browser requirement immediately visible, would reduce scanning without changing the procedure. Evidence: `screens/web/web_recovery_light_375_before.png`.
5. **Medium: demo and future content can borrow too much credibility from polished imagery.** The automotive concept section is visually persuasive and the gauge hero looks like a finished product. Keep the concept/prototype label immediately adjacent to those visuals, and make synthetic or illustrative gauge imagery clear where it represents a rendering rather than a physical observation. This is a presentation recommendation, not a claim that all existing labels are absent. Evidence: `screens/web/web_home_light_1440_before.png`; `screens/esp32/raw/esp32_critical-live_dark_240.png`; `screens/esp32/raw/esp32_actions-hot-blocked_dark_240.png`.
6. **Low: dense mobile hardware copy delays the install action.** The 375-pixel hardware section repeats board and purchasing details across adjacent blocks before its firmware/App CTA. A short requirements summary above that CTA and the existing detailed text below could improve scanning without weakening the unsupported-development qualification. Evidence: `screens/web/web_home_hardware_light_375_before.png`.

The responsive hero retains a clear headline, primary CTA and readable gauge at the inspected widths; the desktop three-up App comparison is easy to scan. Gauge render examples show clear numerical and status hierarchy at native 240 pixels, but cannot establish physical legibility or color appearance. Framed SVGs were not visually rendered in this review because the local SVG conversion dependency was unavailable; raw screenshots were inspected.
